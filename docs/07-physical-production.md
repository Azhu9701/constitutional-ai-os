# Physical Production

Embodied AI makes constitutional architecture concrete.

A robot does not merely produce text. It can consume energy, move objects, alter inventory, affect workers, damage equipment, and produce economic output.

## Independent safety remains mandatory

Constitutional policy is not a replacement for:

- emergency stops;
- certified safety systems;
- motion limits;
- collision avoidance;
- industrial safety procedures.

Physical safety mechanisms should remain independently enforceable.

## Constitutional questions for robotics

A deployment should be able to answer:

- Who owns the robot?
- Who owns the task data?
- Who authorized this task?
- Who may stop it?
- Which human is accountable for deployment?
- Which physical zones are permitted?
- What evidence is required before motion?
- Who receives the output of automated production?
- Who bears the cost of failure?
- How is the deployment policy amended?

## Action path

```text
production objective
→ task plan
→ agent delegation
→ robot capability request
→ constitutional policy
→ safety controller
→ physical action
→ telemetry / receipt
→ world-model update
```

The constitutional layer and the safety controller serve different functions and should both be present.

## Why this matters

Robotics is where abstract questions about AI authority become measurable:

- a permission becomes motion;
- an ownership rule becomes access to a machine;
- a policy error becomes material consequence;
- an audit record becomes an operational history.

For that reason, physical production is an important reference domain for this project.
